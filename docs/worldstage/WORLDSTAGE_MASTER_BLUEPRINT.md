## 1. Executive summary

The public record consistently identifies “Miss Cherry Africa” as **Cherry Pua Africa**, commonly styled as **Coach Cherry**, **Cherry Africa**, historically the **“Girl on Fire,”** and more recently **“The Emotions Expert.”** She is publicly associated with WorldStage International (WSI), Fire University, leadership, coaching, motivational speaking, NLP-related work, transformation, empathy, emotional intelligence, authorship and media/podcast work.

The central product-design conclusion is that WSI should **not** be forced into a generic CRM, LMS, events platform or project-management system. WSI already has a distinctive operating model hidden inside the business:

**Client Dream → Current Reality → Discovery → Design → Fire-Up / Intervention → Evidence → Sustain → Renew**

WSI publicly emphasizes understanding current feelings, mindsets, behaviors, aspirations and organizational reality before designing an intervention; delivering highly tailored experiences; combining head, heart and gut; and then collecting evidence and sustaining change.

The recommended product is therefore a purpose-built **WSI Adaptive OS** — a mobile-first progressive web application and operating layer that learns how Cherry and her team already work. Generic systems such as HubSpot, Zoho, Google Calendar, payment gateways and email/messaging services may remain underneath as commodity systems of record or infrastructure, while the WSI-facing experience should use WorldStage's own language, workflows, roles and intellectual structure.

The highest-value product experiences are:

1. **Cherry Cockpit** — a voice-friendly CEO interface showing only decisions, important relationships, upcoming programs, opportunities at risk and follow-ups requiring Cherry's personal touch.
2. **Discovery-to-Design Copilot** — converts inquiries, “twinkle” conversations, FGDs and approved source material into an evidence-linked client brief, proposed program architecture and draft proposal.
3. **Engagement Studio** — assembles keynotes, team building, Fire University/L&D modules, facilitators, timing, activities, logistics and safety requirements into a run-of-show.
4. **Participant & Client Portals** — mobile/QR onboarding, consent, pre/post pulse surveys, resources, follow-ups and client-facing outcome dashboards.
5. **Sustainment Engine** — coordinates 7/30/90-day follow-ups, pulse checks, action commitments, renewal signals and account intelligence.
6. **Commercial Operations** — CRM sync, proposals, calendars, public-event ticketing, payments, invoicing references and reconciliation.

The primary object in the system should **not** be “event.” It should be **client transformation**. An event is one delivery moment within a larger transformation relationship.

## 2. Project identity and goal

### Purpose
Build a highly personalized business operating system for WorldStage International that simplifies how Cherry Africa and her team run the business while preserving WSI's bespoke, relational, emotionally intelligent way of working.

### Product thesis
The system should feel as though it has **learned WorldStage**, not as though WorldStage has been retrained to use software.

### Strategic goals

- Preserve and scale WSI's bespoke discovery and design process.
- Capture organizational knowledge that currently lives in conversations, people's heads, files and disconnected systems.
- Reduce administrative load on Cherry and senior staff.
- Give Cherry a simple executive command surface rather than a database-like dashboard.
- Turn client discovery into reusable but traceable institutional knowledge.
- Turn program outcomes into an auditable Evidence Ledger.
- Maintain long-term relationship memory across years and staff changes.
- Improve proposal, preparation, delivery, follow-up and renewal speed without making the service generic.
- Make mobile the primary operational surface.
- Use AI as an assistant with evidence, permissions and human approval — not an uncontrolled decision-maker.
- Build privacy, safety, authorization and auditability into the architecture from the beginning.

## 5. WorldStage's actual transformation workflow

The most important domain model is:

**Lead → Relationship → Discovery → Diagnosis → Solution Design → Proposal / Engagement → Pre-work → Program Production → Delivery → Measurement → Client Debrief → Sustainment → Renewal / Expansion**

A simpler WSI-native form is:

**Client Dream → Current Reality → Discovery → Design → Fire-Up → Evidence → Sustain → Renew**

### Discovery

WSI's published process emphasizes discovery/FGDs and goal setting before training. Cherry's keynote process is similarly specific, including a short “twinkle” vision conversation followed by a focused group discussion before tailoring an intervention.

The Discovery Studio should capture:

- business objective;
- desired future state;
- current observable behavior;
- stakeholder language;
- barriers;
- leadership concerns;
- team energy/morale themes;
- previous interventions;
- sensitive topics;
- target behaviors;
- target measures;
- unresolved questions;
- source provenance.

### Design

The Program Composer should model:

`Transformation Objective → Audience → Narrative Arc → Module → Story → Exercise → Tool → Facilitator → Timing → Risk Control → Measurement → Follow-up`

This enables reuse without turning WSI into a cookie-cutter provider.

### Delivery

Delivery should be supported by a phone-optimized Session Run Mode capable of working with poor/intermittent connectivity.

A minimal facilitator stage view:

- **NOW** — current activity
- **NEXT** — next segment/materials
- **TIME** — planned vs. actual
- **SIGNAL** — facilitator/client note
- **HELP** — escalation and safety contacts

### Evidence and sustainment

The system should support:

`Before → Immediately After → 7 days → 30 days → 90 days`

with client-specific measures plus reusable question libraries.

The client dashboard should tell a story:

**What we heard → What we designed → What happened → What changed → What remains → Recommended next move**

## 8. Evidence Ledger

WorldStage publicly publishes strong case-study narratives and numerical outcomes. The system must distinguish company-reported claims from independently validated evidence.

### Evidence state model

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anecdotal | Facilitator/client comment, not formally measured |
| Observed | Structured observation without formal baseline |
| Measured | Defined baseline and follow-up metric exist |
| Client-confirmed | Client sponsor reviewed the result |
| Externally verified | Independent evidence exists |
| Publicly approved | Client/contract permits marketing use |

### Evidence record

`Outcome Claim → Metric Definition → Baseline → Follow-up → Sample → Calculation → Source Artifact → Client Approval → Public-use Permission`

Only claims with the appropriate permission should be available to proposal/content automation for public use.

## 10. Privacy and data-protection architecture

WSI may process unusually intimate workplace information: employee names, opinions, organizational conflicts, emotional/pulse responses, facilitator observations, assessments, participant identity and potentially limited safety-related information.

### Core principles

- lawful, purpose-specific processing;
- data minimization;
- least privilege;
- role-based authorization;
- organizational isolation;
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- audit logs;
- retention controls;
- deletion/export workflows;
- pseudonymization/aggregation where appropriate;
- client/participant privacy by design;
- no indiscriminate AI access;
- no external-model training on confidential client data by default;
- explicit governance of automated profiling/AI use.

### Required separation

- Sales role cannot see individual FGD responses.
- Client sponsor cannot see identifiable responses intended to be anonymous.
- Facilitator receives only data necessary for the specific session.
- Content AI cannot search participant records.
- Analytics receives pseudonymous telemetry, not discovery transcripts.
- General AI assistants must not receive restricted health/safety notes.
- Finance sees payment state, not participant sentiment.

### Participant analytics guardrails

Default to cohort-level aggregation and minimum cohort sizes for breakdowns. Do not turn pulse data into employee-ranking or mental-health profiling.

The AI layer must not autonomously label people as “low empathy,” “resistant,” “negative,” “high risk,” or make mental-health diagnoses based on participation, faces, voices or private responses.

### Sensitive safety information

Where an experiential activity genuinely requires health/accessibility information:

- collect only the minimum necessary operational answer;
- isolate it in a restricted store;
- document purpose and access;
- define a short retention policy with privacy/legal guidance;
- do not copy detailed diagnoses into general CRM records.

### Experience layer

- Cherry Mobile Cockpit
- WSI Team Console

- Public Website / Events

### WSI Adaptive OS layer

- Application API
- Authorization/RBAC
- Workflow/automation engine
- AI orchestration and guardrails
- Approved WSI Knowledge Vault
- Evidence Ledger
- Operational PostgreSQL database
- Encrypted document storage
- Audit log

### External/commodity services

Potentially:

- CRM: HubSpot or Zoho
- Calendar: Google Calendar
- Payments: PayMongo and/or Maya
- Messaging: WhatsApp Business / email
- Transactional email provider
- Product analytics with aggressive privacy controls
- Accounting/BIR-compatible invoicing
- AI providers behind a WSI-owned abstraction layer

### Technology direction

A managed PostgreSQL backend is conceptually well suited because WSI's domain is highly relational. A Supabase-style architecture is a strong candidate because it combines PostgreSQL, auth integration, storage and Row Level Security while reducing custom infrastructure burden.

A Vercel-hosted PWA/front end can provide rapid global delivery, but deployment edge location is not equivalent to database/storage residency; data-location and enterprise-client requirements must be verified before production.

## 27. Go / no-go proof gates

### Core application

Not “done” because it deploys. Ready only when:

- role/permission tests prove restricted discovery/participant data cannot cross boundaries;
- backup restoration has been tested;
- rollback path exists;
- calendar/payment webhook failure and replay paths work;
- mobile behavior is checked on real phones;
- privacy notices/consent versions are approved;
- critical workflows work end to end.

### AI

Not ready because summaries look impressive. Ready only when:

- factual-grounding test corpus passes agreed threshold;
- source attribution is enforced;
- privacy isolation is proven;
- refusal behavior works;
- consequential actions require approval;
- model/prompt/version and relevant sources are auditable.

### Participant analytics

Not ready because dashboards render. Ready only when:

- purposes are documented;
- anonymity thresholds are approved;
- retention is approved;
- client visibility rules are approved;
- re-identification risks are tested.

### Checkout/payments

Not ready because sandbox returns “success.” Ready only after:

- real low-value production transaction;
- confirmation;
- webhook processing;
- reconciliation;
- customer-safe failure state;
- refund/cancellation path;
- duplicate/replay protection;
- monitoring/alerting;
- rollback/recovery evidence.

## 32. Final product vision

The mature product is not simply “an app for a training company.” It is a digital expression of WorldStage's method.

### Before the room
Remember the relationship, listen deeply, understand the gap and design personally.

### Inside the room
Give facilitators everything they need while keeping technology nearly invisible.

### After the room
Capture what happened, show credible evidence, sustain commitments and remember the relationship years later.

### For Cherry
Remove administrative friction while preserving exactly what should not be automated: **judgment, storytelling, empathy, energy and human connection.**

The software should follow the same rule WorldStage applies to its clients: **no cookie-cutter intervention.**
